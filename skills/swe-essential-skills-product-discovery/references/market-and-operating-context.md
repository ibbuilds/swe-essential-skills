# Market and operating context

Use for market-facing discovery or when location materially changes the problem, feasible alternatives or investment decision. Keep the analysis tied to the user's actual project and target situation. It is not a mandatory business plan for an internal utility or a settled implementation task.

## Establish the setting before extrapolating

Recover the intended outcome, current problem, target segment, user/buyer/approver roles, operating geography and reach from the accepted brief and evidence. Where users live, where delivery occurs and where the organization/developer is based can differ. Do not infer the market from a language, timezone, folder name or a convenient national dataset.

Check only conditions that can change the decision: local workflows, purchasing power and actual prices, language/accessibility, connectivity/devices, trust, distribution, payments/currency, migration/interoperability, procurement, regulation or physical delivery. Ground material claims in the relevant population, jurisdiction, date and operating environment. Ask for a missing location/segment only when it changes the analysis; continue independent work without pretending the unknown is settled.

An official statistic from another country is not local demand. A national population total is not the reachable customer base. Preserve denominators, selection, time windows and geographic scope when assessing prevalence or opportunity. Distinguish observed demand from a proposed market-size estimate; do not invent a revenue forecast to make the project attractive.

## Search competition at the relevant levels

For a market opportunity, investigate local, national and international candidates, plus indirect substitutes that solve the same problem. Preserve explicitly requested geographic coverage. Organize discovery around the problem, target segment and real purchasing/use alternatives, rather than identical interface features or a list of famous brands.

| Level | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Local | Providers and existing practices available to the target area/segment, including offline services and manual processes. |
| National | Relevant firms/platforms in the operating country; verify service-area and segment coverage instead of assuming national branding includes the target locality. |
| International | Foreign/global products that can reach the same customers, plus informative foreign-market analogues. Separate actual reachable competitors from examples unavailable in the operating market. |
| Substitutes | Spreadsheets, messaging, outsourcing, in-house tools, existing libraries and doing nothing. These can determine switching costs even when nobody calls them competitors. |

A foreign online provider may be a direct local competitor; origin alone does not exclude it. An unavailable foreign product may inform an approach without establishing current local competition or local demand.

Use suitable connected sources and targeted public research. Prefer provider documentation for current coverage, prices and requirements; use relevant independent evidence for adoption, quality and comparative claims. Verify local constraints through applicable authoritative sources and people with demonstrated relevant expertise. Marketing claims and search summaries are leads, not independent proof.

## Compare fit and preserve uncertainty

For each material alternative, establish the problem/segment it serves, actual availability, current method and value, material price/operating constraints, switching barriers and source/date. Use a compact table only when useful. Do not require an arbitrary number of companies or fill unavailable fields with guesses.

Explain what the proposed project would improve relative to those real alternatives and what supports that claim. Include contrary evidence: a cheap existing method may solve the problem, demand may be concentrated elsewhere, or a delivery/payment constraint may invalidate the proposed approach. A polished interface or an owner's enthusiasm does not establish an unmet need, viable acquisition or willingness to pay.

Record coverage and gaps accurately. An empty query, inaccessible source or incomplete directory does not prove an uncontested market. Broaden relevant terminology/scope when needed; do not claim exhaustive competition research or absence from a first page. If requested coverage cannot be completed, name the missing part and its effect on the decision.

Conclude for this project, segment and place: evidence-supported opportunity, existing solution, narrower audience, bounded next check, or insufficient case for investment. Keep recommendations distinct from owner decisions. Respect an explicit decision to build while exposing material contrary evidence; do not manufacture favorable conclusions or replace the chosen project silently.
